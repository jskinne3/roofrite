# Navigating the Roof Insurance Claim Process

After a storm, looking up at your roof can bring on a heavy dose of stress. 

_Did a stray limb crack a shingle? Did that heavy hail cause dents? Is it time to call my insurance agent?_

Let's break down exactly how the roof insurance claim process works, the common pitfalls to avoid, and why your choice of a roofing contractor makes all the difference in securing a fair payout.

## Step 1: Spotting the Signs of Valid Roof Damage

Before you pick up the phone to call your insurance company, it pays to do a little [ground-level detective work](https://roofriteexteriors.com/blog/Ground-Level-Detective/). Filing a claim for very minor issues can sometimes raise your premiums unnecessarily, so you want to ensure you have a valid case.

* **Check Outside (Safely!):** Grab a pair of binoculars and walk around your yard. Look for missing, cracked, or lifted shingles. Check your gutters for dents or an accumulation of shingle granules (which look like coarse black sand), as these are classic telltale signs of hail damage.
* **Look for Clues Inside:** Head up to the attic with a flashlight. Are there dark water stains on the wood, damp insulation, or beams of light peeking through? Check your living area ceilings for yellowish-brown water circles or bubbling paint, too.

If you spot these issues, you are likely looking at a valid structural claim rather than just a cosmetic fix.

## Step 2: Shift Into "Documentation Mode"

When it comes to insurance claims, proof is your best friend. The more detailed evidence you possess, the smoother your claim will go.

* **Take Clear Photos and Videos:** Capture wide shots of your entire home to show context, then zoom in for close-ups of specific problem areas like hail dents, lifted shingles, or interior leaks.
* **Keep Detailed Written Records:** Note the date and time of the storm. Start a log of every conversation you have with your insurance company, including names, dates, and promises made. Keep receipts for any emergency temporary fixes, like throwing a tarp over a leak.

## Step 3: Understanding the Filing Process & Your Policy

Once you report the damage to your insurer’s claims department, they will assign you a claim number and schedule an insurance adjuster to inspect your property. Before they arrive, it is vital to check your policy type:

* **Replacement Cost Value (RCV):** This is the gold standard. It covers the full cost to replace your roof with similar materials at today's market prices (minus your deductible). RCV is usually paid out in two checks: one up-front for the depreciated value, and a final check once the work is completed.
* **Actual Cash Value (ACV):** This policy only pays for what your roof was worth right before the storm, factoring in its age and depreciation.

Knowing your policy structure—and filing within your policy's strict deadlines (usually one year)—ensures you won't be left with a financial surprise.
